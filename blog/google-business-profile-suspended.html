Guide for HVAC & plumbing contractors

Google Business Profile Suspended? Read This Before You Appeal.

Google gives you one appeal, then one request for additional review. Most of the work happens before you file.

Updated November 10, 2026

How to tell it's a suspension

Your dashboard shows "suspended" or "restricted", or Google emailed that the profile broke its guidelines.
Customers can't find you on Maps, even searching your exact business name.
Calls from Google and Local Services Ads drop off at the same time.

A listing that's only ranking lower is a different problem. Check the dashboard first.

Why Google suspends contractor profiles

HVAC and plumbing shops are service-area businesses: you go to the customer. Most contractor suspensions trace back to Google's rules for that kind of business.

Address shown, no storefront

If customers don't come to you, hide the address and show only the service area.

Virtual office or mailbox

Google doesn't accept a PO box, mailbox store or virtual office as the address.

Service area too wide

Google's guideline is about two hours' drive from where you're based.

Keywords in the name

"Best HVAC Kansas City" when that isn't the name on your license.

A recent change

A move, new phone number, category or name change can trigger a fresh review of the whole profile.

A second profile

Google says not to create a new profile while one is suspended.

Fix the cause first. An appeal for a profile that still breaks the rule gets denied, and that uses up your attempt.

The documents Google accepts

Google lists official business registration, a business license, tax certificates, and business utility bills (electric, phone, water, internet). Your trade license helps show the business is real.

The business name and address on every document must match your profile exactly. This is where most appeals fail: the license says "LLC" and the profile doesn't, or the utility bill has an old suite number.

How the appeal works

1Appeal from Google's Business Profile appeals tool, signed in to the account that owns the profile. Google's instructions.
2Attach your documents and explain what you fixed.
3If it's denied, you get one more request for additional review, with new evidence.

If the whole Google account is restricted, not just the profile, lift that restriction first, then appeal the profile.

How long it takes right now

Google posted on October 15, 2026 that review-related appeals are running slow because of high volume, and that it can't give a timeframe. Owners on Google's help forum report appeals sitting for weeks. Google's notice.

Google also says not to file duplicate appeals. Extra filings don't speed it up.
