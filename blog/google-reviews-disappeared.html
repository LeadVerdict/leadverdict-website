Google Reviews Disappeared? Here's What Comes Back.

Some missing reviews are only delayed. Some are gone for good. A few were removed by mistake, and those are worth fighting for.

Published November 12, 2026

First, check that they're really gone

A new review doesn't always show up right away. Google says that in some cases the check before a review goes live "might take a few days." Reviews can also go missing for a while after two profiles are merged, until both sets show.

Search your business name on Google and on Maps while signed out, not from the owner dashboard.
Ask the customer to look for their review on their own Google account, so you know whether it was posted at all.
Check your email for a notice from Google about your profile.

Source: Google's page About missing or delayed reviews.

Why Google removes reviews

Google says reviews are usually removed for policy violations like spam or inappropriate content. For contractors, these rules catch real reviews most often:

Offering something for a review

A discount, a gift card or a free filter change in exchange for a review counts as an incentivized review. Google removes them.

Asking only happy customers

Google bans selectively asking for positive reviews, or steering unhappy customers away from posting.

Reviews from people close to you

Employees, family and business partners have a conflict of interest. Their reviews get removed.

Reviews you paid for

Paid reviews, from any vendor, count as fake engagement. That is the violation that brings the strongest penalties.

Source: Google Maps prohibited and restricted content policy.

Which reviews come back

Google says reviews removed for policy violations won't be restored. Reporting or appealing doesn't bring them back.

The exception is a mistake. Google admits its automated spam detection can remove legitimate reviews, and tells owners to contact support when that happens. When you do, bring a list: the reviewer's name, the job date, the invoice number, and a screenshot of the review if you have one. A review tied to a real, documented job is the strongest case you can make.

When the whole profile gets a review restriction

If Google decides an owner broke the fake engagement policy, it can go beyond removing reviews. Google lists these examples:

The profile can't receive new reviews or ratings for a set period.
Existing reviews and ratings are unpublished for a set period.
The profile shows customers a warning that fake reviews were removed.

Google says it emails owners before applying a restriction, and that you can appeal. It doesn't say how long a restriction lasts. Source: Business Profile restrictions for policy violations.

If you never bought reviews and a restriction hit anyway, the appeal needs to show where your reviews actually came from: how you ask for them, and which jobs they match.

How long it takes right now

Google posted on October 15, 2026 that review-related appeals are taking longer than usual because of high volume. Google's notice. Don't file the same appeal twice; it doesn't move you up the queue.

What to stop doing today

Stop any discount or giveaway tied to leaving a review.
Ask every customer for a review, not only the happy ones.
Don't let staff or family review the business.
Don't hire anyone who sells reviews or promises to remove them.
