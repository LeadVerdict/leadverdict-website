Fake One-Star Reviews? Report Them the Right Way.

Google gives you one appeal per review. Most of the work is picking the right policy and having proof before you file.

Published November 12, 2026

What Google will remove, and what it won't

Google only removes reviews that break its policies. Its own help page says not to report a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it. A harsh but real review from a real customer stays up.

These are the categories that fit most fake one-stars against contractors:

Fake engagement

The reviewer was never a customer, or the review was posted to drag your rating down.

Conflict of interest

A competitor, a former employee or someone with a business dispute reviewing you.

Off-topic

The review isn't about an experience with your business: politics, a different company, a rant about something else.

Harassment or hate

Threats, slurs or personal attacks on you or your techs.

Source: Google Maps prohibited and restricted content policy.

Before you report

Search your job records for the reviewer's name, phone and address. "No matching job" is the most useful fact you can have.
Screenshot the review with the date showing. Reviews can be edited or deleted later.
Note anything that ties it to a competitor or ex-employee: same week as a dispute, details only an insider would know.

Then reply publicly, short and calm: you have no record of a job under that name and you'd like them to call the office. Customers reading the reviews see the reply too. You need a verified profile to reply.

How to report it

1Open your Business Profile, select Read reviews, and select Report next to the review.
2Pick the reason that matches the policy it breaks, then select Send report.
3Or use Google's Reviews Management Tool and choose "Report a new review for removal". Google's instructions.

Tracking the report

Google says the evaluation typically takes several days. In the Reviews Management Tool, each report shows one of three statuses:

Decision pending

Still in the queue. Right now that can mean weeks, not days.

Report reviewed - no policy violation

Google kept the review up. This is where the appeal comes in.

Escalated - check your email

It went to a further review. Watch your inbox.

Google posted on October 15, 2026 that review-related appeals are taking longer than usual because of high volume. Google's notice.

The one-time appeal

If Google decides a review doesn't break policy, you get one appeal per review. Use it once, with your evidence ready.

In the Reviews Management Tool, choose "Check the status of a review I reported previously and appeal options", then "Appeal eligible reviews". You can select up to 10 reviews at a time. Google sends the result by email.

What makes it worse

Posting fake five-stars to bury the one-star. That's fake engagement too, and it puts your own profile at risk.
Reporting the same review over and over. It doesn't speed anything up.
Paying anyone who guarantees removal. Only Google, or the person who wrote the review, can take it down.
Arguing with the reviewer in the reply. Future customers read it.
