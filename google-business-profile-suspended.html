Google Business Profile suspended?

Read this before you hit appeal. Google gives you one appeal, then one request for additional review, so most of the work happens before you file.

Updated November 10, 2026

How to tell it's a suspension

  • Your profile shows "suspended" or "restricted" in your Business Profile dashboard, or you got an email saying it broke Google's guidelines.
  • Customers can't find you on Maps, even when they search your exact business name.
  • Calls from Google and Local Services Ads drop off at the same time.

A listing that is just ranking lower is a different problem. Check the dashboard before you treat it as a suspension.

Why Google suspends contractor profiles

HVAC and plumbing shops are service-area businesses: you go to the customer. Most contractor suspensions trace back to Google's rules for that kind of business:

  • Showing an address with no storefront. If customers don't come to you, the address should be hidden and only the service area shown.
  • A virtual office, PO box or mailbox store as the address. Google doesn't accept these for a profile.
  • A service area that's too wide. Google's guideline is about two hours' drive from where you're based.
  • Extra words in the business name, like "Best HVAC Kansas City", when that isn't the name on your license.
  • A recent change: a move, a new phone number, a category change, or a name change. Edits can trigger a fresh review of the whole profile.
  • A second profile for the same business. Google says not to create a new profile while one is suspended.

Fix the cause first. An appeal for a profile that still breaks the rule gets denied, and that uses up your attempt.

The documents Google accepts

Google lists official business registration, a business license, tax certificates, and business utility bills (electric, phone, water, internet). Your trade license helps show the business is real.

The business name and address on every document must match your profile exactly. This is where most appeals fail: the license says "LLC", the profile doesn't, or the utility bill has an old suite number.

How the appeal works

  • You appeal from Google's Business Profile appeals tool, signed in to the account that owns the profile. Google's instructions.
  • You can attach your documents and explain what you fixed.
  • If the appeal is denied, you get one more request for additional review, with new evidence.
  • If the whole Google account is restricted, not just the profile, lift that restriction first. Then appeal the profile.

How long it takes right now

Google posted on October 15, 2026 that review-related appeals are running slow because of high volume, and that it can't give a timeframe. Owners on Google's help forum report appeals sitting for weeks. Google's notice.

Google also says not to file duplicate appeals. Once it's in, extra filings don't speed it up.
